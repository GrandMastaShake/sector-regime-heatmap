# 2026-09-21 - The owner's watchlist, and gold, the dollar and bitcoin beside the tape

Two owner decisions of 2026-09-21. They are recorded together because both
land on the daily tape.

## Decision 1: the baskets follow the owner's own watchlist

### What changed

- The master for `config/watchlist_110.csv` is now **the owner's own Finviz
  watchlist**. Until today the CSV was a transcription of
  `Seven_Orbs_Watchlist_110.xlsx`. The filename stays, for continuity; the
  count lives in `config/watchlist.yaml` (`universe_size: 109`, file v2).
- **AVB is removed.** AvalonBay was never on the owner's list. It was added to
  give Real Estate a tenth name, and it has since merged into Vivmark
  Residential and stopped trading on 2026-08-18. Real Estate now holds nine
  names. The other ten sectors keep their ten, and no other row changed -- not
  the sectors, not the undated market caps.
- **BTC and GLD are on the owner's list and are not sector members.** They are
  in no basket; they are comparison rows on the tape (decision 2).
- `config/sector_baskets.yaml` was regenerated by `scripts/sync_baskets.py`.

### How the export was verified

The master copy is a Finviz screener export, `finviz (3).csv`, in the owner's
Downloads folder: 111 data rows under the columns `No., Ticker, Company,
Sector, Industry, Country, Market Cap, P/E, Price, Change, Volume`, SHA-256
`800c9eaf755ea7386435f3348d1b492a11dec222af0323856d18eccf985eeedf`.

- Its 111 tickers are unique, and they are exactly the previous
  `watchlist_110.csv` without AVB, plus BTC and GLD. Finviz files those two
  under sector "Financial", industry "Exchange Traded Fund".
- The same list is committed upstream as weekly-council-scan
  `scan_pipeline/config/council_watchlist.csv` (commit `8ef6f33`, blob
  `0111b7ef`): the same 111 tickers, and its sector for every one of the 109
  stocks matches this repo's. It files BTC and GLD as "Macro Assets".
- Finviz's own sector labels were **not** used. They are Finviz's taxonomy:
  different names for four sectors ("Consumer Cyclical", "Consumer Defensive",
  "Financial", "Basic Materials") and a different sector for three stocks --
  FSLR (Technology there, Energy here), MOD (Consumer Cyclical there,
  Industrials here) and GOOGL (Communication Services there, Technology here).
  The owner kept every remaining row as it was.

The export is not committed. It carries prices, P/E and volume as of its pull,
which are not this repo's data, and the one thing taken from it -- the ticker
set -- is verified above.

### What it changes in published numbers: a denominator, nothing else

AVB was the missing Real Estate name in every published Real Estate block: the
three staged inputs (2026-08-21, 2026-09-11, 2026-09-18) and all seven tapes
(2026-09-10 to 2026-09-18) read 9 of 10, with AVB the one missing. No published
return, breadth, up-volume share, rank or component ever included it.

Going forward Real Estate reads 9 of 9: a pass, where it carried a permanent
warn. Checked by recomputing the 2026-09-18 tape with the new code into a
scratch file (the published tape was not touched): every sector number, rank
and warning is identical; Real Estate's `constituents_expected` goes 10 to 9,
`coverage_pct` 90 to 100, and its `missing` list empties.

Grading is unaffected. `src/evaluate.py` reads the current baskets. For the two
windows closed so far -- cycle 1's month (2026-08-21 to 2026-09-18) and cycle
2's week (2026-09-11 to 2026-09-18) -- the realized Real Estate return with and
without AVB is identical to the thousandth, because AVB has no usable bar at
either end of either window; only the recorded denominator differs (9 of 9
rather than 9 of 10). The windows still open all start on 2026-09-11 or
2026-09-18, where AVB has no bar either.

### The gates

No gate or test assumes a basket size any more. Each reads it from the
watchlist.

- `tests/test_watchlist.py`: "110 unique tickers" and "exactly ten per sector"
  became: tickers unique; the count equals the declared `universe_size`;
  eleven sectors; each basket as large as its sector's rows in the CSV; each
  basket at or above the data-quality floor.
- `scripts/preflight.py`: `check_basket_floor` fails a basket listed below
  `MIN_CONSTITUENTS` -- it would fail data quality on every run.
  `check_comparisons_are_not_sectors` fails GLD, BTC or DXY in the CSV or in a
  basket, which is what copying the owner's list in wholesale would do.
- `scripts/stage_run.py`: the refusal printed "below 8 of 10" and "7/10"
  whatever the basket held; it now prints each sector's own used/expected.
- `scripts/render_dashboard.py`: the SPCX constraint's "denominator of 9" is
  now the Industrials basket's size less one. Still 9; the README dashboard
  block is unchanged.
- `tests/test_compute_metrics.py`: the fixtures that planted the zero-volume
  bar on AVB now plant it on a live Real Estate name. With AVB in no basket
  they would have passed without testing anything. Counts are derived.

What did not move:

- **The floor.** Under 8 usable names is `data_quality: fail` and a staging
  refusal, as before. A test pins 8 in all three places that enforce it
  (`compute_metrics.MIN_CONSTITUENTS`, `stage_run.MIN_COVERAGE`,
  `make_sector_inputs.data_quality`).
- **The tolerance, counted in live names.** Before, Real Estate had nine live
  names and a dead tenth: one more missing was a warn, two a fail. Now it has
  nine: one missing is a warn, two a fail. The only difference is that none
  missing reads pass rather than warn.
- `universe_size` stays a declared number rather than a derived one, so a
  dropped CSV row is caught instead of silently regenerated into a smaller
  basket.
- Eleven GICS sectors. Weights, bands, formulas and every other threshold.

## Decision 2: gold, the dollar and bitcoin beside the tape

### What they are

Three comparison rows, computed by `src/macro_comparisons.py` into their own
`macro_comparisons` block of the daily tape artifact. For each instrument: its
return over the tape's own windows -- 1 and 5 sessions, the same sessions the
sectors are read over -- and that return minus SPY's over the same window,
with the two closes it was computed from.

| Row | Read from | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | `series.GLD` | SPDR Gold Shares |
| US dollar | `fx.DXY` | US Dollar Index, the council panel's fx block |
| Bitcoin | `series.BTC` | Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust ETF |

The relative figure is the same subtraction as a sector's
`sector_return_vs_spy_pct`, but it is named `return_vs_spy_pct` so that nothing
reading sector fields can pick it up.

### What they are not

- **Not sectors.** No basket, so no breadth; no volume confirmation (DXY has
  no volume at all); no rank among the eleven; no score, band or rating. The
  block says `is_sector: false`, and a test walks every key in it and fails on
  any score, band, rating, breadth, volume, component, rank, constituent,
  confidence or judgment-component key.
- **Not an input.** Never in a forecast, an input payload or an evaluation.
  That is held by structure, not by care: the block is its own top-level key,
  so everything that walks `sectors` -- the cross-sector ranking,
  `regime_prior.compare`, the README chart -- cannot meet a row; nothing on the
  forecast path imports the module (a test reads `compute_metrics`,
  `make_sector_inputs`, `assemble_payload`, `heatmap`, `evaluate` and
  `stage_run`); the weekly metrics carry no such block even when the weekly
  panel holds GLD, BTC and DXY; and the assembler refuses a staged "Gold"
  sector file.
- **Not in the sector chart.** The README renders them as a separate markdown
  table under the chart. Inside it they would share its bar scale -- one
  volatile row would flatten eleven sector bars -- and its BRDTH and UPVOL
  columns, which a comparison row does not have.

### Missing is missing

- **DXY** is in the daily panel's `fx` block from 2026-09-11. The bootstrap
  files 2026-08-24 to 2026-09-10 carry `fx: {}`, so a window starting on one
  of them has no dollar return.
- **GLD and BTC** are not in the panel yet. weekly-council-scan added both to
  its price feed on 2026-09-21 (`8ef6f33`, 319 to 321 series), and the owner
  expects them in `series` from the 2026-09-25 build. A 1-session value needs
  a bar on two sessions and a 5-session value on six: if the first bar is
  2026-09-25's, the first day value is on the 2026-09-28 tape and the first
  week value on 2026-10-02's.
- Until then each window reads `missing`, with the reason -- the panel's, plus
  the feed's own `missing` entry when it has one. The README cell says
  `missing`.
- **No proxy.** The `commodities` block's GOLD future is a different
  instrument. A test puts it in every session with GLD absent and requires the
  gold row to stay missing. The sources are constants in the module, not
  configuration, because a configurable source is how a proxy gets in.
- **Inherited gates.** A GLD or BTC bar with zero volume is rejected exactly as
  for a basket name. A move beyond the tape's 25% threshold is flagged in the
  block's own `warnings` and retained.

On basis: SPY is total-return adjusted like every `yahoo-daily` series; GLD and
BTC pay no distributions, so their adjusted and raw closes coincide; DXY is an
index level. The relative figure is the subtraction the sectors already use.

Checked against the real panel: the new code over weekly-council-scan's
`data/daily` through 2026-09-18, written to a scratch file rather than
`data/tape/`, reads DXY 100.236 to 100.215 over one session (-0.021%) and
99.095 to 100.215 over five (+1.130%), and GLD and BTC as "absent from series
at 2026-09-18".

## A published tape is never rewritten

This is the first change that makes a recompute of an already-published date
differ from what was published: the new block, and Real Estate's denominator.
The scheduled job recomputes the latest tape whenever upstream has no new
session, and `daily_tape.py` overwrote unconditionally -- so the first such run
after this lands would have rewritten `data/tape/2026-09-18.json` in place.

`daily_tape.py` now compares before it writes. Identical content (newlines
normalised, since a Windows checkout holds CRLF) is a no-op and exits 0,
exactly as before. Different content is refused, exit 2, and the file is left
as it was. A differing recompute means the code or the panel moved underneath a
published record; an upstream `.corrected.json` would do it too, and used to be
absorbed silently. The job now fails loudly on such a run instead, until the
next session gives it a new date to write.

The seven published tapes are unchanged. The README renders the latest of them
with every comparison row missing, and says that tape predates the rows.
