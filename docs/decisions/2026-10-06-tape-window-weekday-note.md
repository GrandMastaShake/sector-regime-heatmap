# 2026-10-06 - The tape block says when a window holds more weekdays than sessions

## What changed

`scripts/render_tape.py` now holds each window of the tape it renders to the
calendar, in the one way a tape can. It counts the weekdays after
`window_from` up to and including `window_to`, and compares that with the
sessions the window is labelled as: 1 for the day horizon, 5 for the week.
When there are more weekdays than sessions it adds one line to the README
block, above the chart and under the session count. For
`data/tape/2026-10-05.json`, the newest tape:

    More weekdays than sessions. The 1d window runs from the 2026-09-23 close
    to the 2026-10-05 close: 8 weekdays, 1 session, so at least 7 weekdays in
    it have no session in the panel. The 5d window runs from the 2026-09-16
    close to the 2026-10-05 close: 13 weekdays, 5 sessions, so at least 8
    weekdays in it have no session in the panel. A weekday without a session
    is a market holiday or a session the feed never wrote, and the panel
    cannot tell which.

When the counts are equal the block is exactly what it was.

That is the whole change. `src/daily_tape.py` and `src/macro_comparisons.py`
are untouched, no artifact gained a field, nothing under `data/` was written,
and no weight, band, threshold or formula moved. The README block was
regenerated by the script.

This is option 2 of the three in `2026-10-05-weekly-window-contiguity.md`, in
its renderer form, which the owner asked to have built on 2026-10-05. It is a
disclosure, not a fix. Options 1 and 3 are still open, and this stands in for
neither.

The last column is read from the daily panel at upstream `7069b2f`, the commit

on the 2026-09-22 tape, six on 2026-09-23, eight and thirteen on 2026-10-05 --
and the last two are the ones on the page. Until this change the README gave
gold as -3.39% and bitcoin as +1.39% under `1d`, for a move from the
2026-09-23 close to the 2026-10-05 close, and drew the eleven sectors under
`5d vs SPY` over 2026-09-16..2026-10-05. Nothing in the block said so. A
window's two ends are in the artifact and were not rendered.

## What a tape can prove, and what the note says

A window reads the close on `window_to` against the close on `window_from`.
Its sessions are on the days after its first date, its last date included,
and a session cannot fall on a weekend.

- **As many weekdays as sessions: the window is whole.** Every weekday in it
  is a session the panel holds, so none is missing. Nothing is rendered.
- **More weekdays than sessions: a weekday in it has no session file.** At
  least the difference between the two counts. The note says "at least"
  because the count takes nothing about the files on trust: a session dated
  on a weekend, or filed twice, would leave more weekdays without one, never
  fewer.
- **Why it has none is not in the panel.** The market was closed that day, or
  the feed never wrote it. The first row of the table and the sixth agree on
  every count: eight calendar days, six weekdays, five sessions, one weekday
  unfiled. One is Labor Day and one is not.

So the note gives both counts, says how many weekdays at least have no session
in the panel, names both readings and picks neither. On the 2026-09-10 tape it
reads word for word as it does on 2026-09-23, but for the dates. A test holds
the two shapes to the same words.

It will appear across every market holiday: on the five tapes whose week
window holds the holiday, and for the day window on the first of them. That is
not a fault report and it is not noise to tune out. It is the block declining
to call a window five sessions when it cannot show it.

## Why the renderer and not the artifact

Putting the two counts into the tape itself was the other form of this option.
It was not taken.

- **It changes the bytes of every tape.** The scheduled job recomputes the
  newest session on each run, and `daily_tape.py` refuses, exit 2, when the
  bytes differ from the published file. The job would have failed on
  2026-10-05 until upstream posted a later session, and every published tape
  would from then on disagree with what the code computes for its date.
- **It could not reach the tapes that need it.** A published tape is never
  rewritten, so the five long windows would have stayed unmarked.
- **The renderer needs nothing new.** `window_from` and `window_to` have been
  in every sector block since the first tape, so the note works on all ten,
  the seven that predate the comparison rows included. The comparison rows
  take the same windows from the same session list and are not read a second
  time.

The cost is that the disclosure lives on the README, and only for the tape it
renders. `data/tape/2026-09-22.json` still says nothing about its own windows,
and anything that reads tapes directly gets no flag.

## What it does not do

- **It consults no exchange calendar,** and none was added. Upstream's writer
  waits for a later session as proof of a holiday rather than consult one, and
  its offline gate knows no holidays. A list kept here would be this repo's own
  opinion of which days were sessions, with nothing in its panel to check it
  against, and a list of holidays is not a list of sessions held. Without one
  the note cannot be quieter on Labor Day than on a lost session. It is not
  meant to be.
- **It refuses nothing.** A tape whose window is long is still computed,
  published and rendered, and no exit code changed. Refusing means knowing
  which weekdays were sessions. That is option 1: upstream writes each daily
  file's previous session from its witness, and the tape checks the chain.
- **It does not name the weekday.** A tape carries the two ends of a window
  and not the sessions between them, and the renderer reads committed
  artifacts only. The dates in the table above came from the panel.
- **It says nothing when a window has fewer weekdays than sessions.** That
  cannot happen while every session is its own weekday. It would take a file
  dated on a weekend or twice, and the panel has neither.
- **It backfills nothing.** The five windows stay as published.

A week window reads across a gap on the first five tapes after it, and
2026-10-05 is the first. With nothing further lost, the next four tapes each
add a long week window and carry the note, unless upstream recovers the lost
sessions first.
